The Deeper Frame

Two frameworks that explain not just what happened — but why it keeps happening, and what it means at a scale larger than any single statute.

01

The Pen and the Institution

How the first act of writing became the first act of control — and why it never stopped

From the grain inventories of Uruk to the search algorithm, whoever holds the pen decides what exists. This essay traces the knowledge monopoly from its oldest documented form through the colonial pen that rendered Indigenous sovereignty legally invisible — and into the architecture of the present.

02

The Way of Scale

What was lost and why it matters

The suppression of sovereign peoples was not just political. It was a disruption of something operating at a scale larger than colonial administration understood. This essay argues that the Dawes Act, the boarding schools, and the algorithm are all expressions of the same compression — the imposition of a shorter time horizon onto peoples who built knowledge systems for centuries.

03

The Binary Was Not the Beginning

How the dualist model was imposed as universal — and what the record actually shows about what came before it

American Indians came from a unifying model of political status. The Western model is dualist. That model has been forced onto humanity and reinforces itself by saying this is how it has always been. This essay follows the physics of why that claim cannot be true — and why the body already knows it.

Next Steps

What the primary record establishes — and where the argument goes from here

374 ratified treaties. The supreme law of the land. The United States broke every obligation they established. The record of the obligations and the record of the breaches are in the same archive. This essay documents both — and names where the argument goes next.
